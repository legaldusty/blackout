Numbers we measured ourselves

Every number here comes from our own recorded test runs. The conditions are listed under each one.

Not pictured, by choice

Smart glasses and home robots film the people around them and send that video away. Blackout lets those people say no, and makes the camera listen.

  1. The tag speaks twice. Over Bluetooth it says a tag is here and which blink pattern it is using. With infrared light, which people cannot see, it blinks that pattern.
  2. The camera has two eyes. One films the scene. The other sits behind a filter that blocks visible light, so it sees almost nothing except tags.
  3. It matches light to a person. The camera only looks for patterns it heard announced, then finds the person the light is on.
  4. It erases them before anything leaves. The video runs 2.5 seconds behind, so the erase covers the moment before the tag was read.

What we say out loud

  • Only cameras that choose, or are required, to honor the tag will obey it. It is a standard, like a crosswalk signal.
  • The tag hides who you are, not what you do. A silhouette and its actions stay visible.
  • The tag has no serial number. Its pattern and its radio address change every few minutes, so it cannot be used to follow you.
  • Researchers at UC Irvine proposed bystander signals in 2025. Blackout is our working take: radio and light together, a whole-body erase, and a camera that goes black when it is unsure.
